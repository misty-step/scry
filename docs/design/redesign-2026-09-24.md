# Interface redesign, 2026-09-24

On 2026-09-24 the operator judged the v5 interface "pretty atrocious" and asked
for every component, page, and flow to be reviewed, reimagined, and polished
(MIS-162). This record holds the audit, the divergent concepts, the critique,
and the decision. [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md) holds the resulting
specification. Behavior contracts in [SPEC](../../SPEC.md) and the stories in
[USER_STORIES](../../USER_STORIES.md) did not change.

## Method and evidence

The audit used the running Go binary on an isolated exe.dev VM. A loopback fake
stood in for the model, Exa, and Jev, and served authored content. The real
capture chain created five goals with 24 concepts and about 50 questions, plus
failed, partial, and in-progress preparations. A Playwright driver then worked
through the Stream with real form submissions. It captured 45 named states in
both color schemes at 390 px and 1280 px. The screenshots are kept outside Git.

## Audit

### System-wide

1. **The palettes are the two most common generated-UI defaults.** Night is
   near-black with one orange accent. Daylight is warm cream (`#F4F1EA`) with a
   terracotta accent. Neither says anything about studying or about Scry.
2. **Nothing has a distinct weight.** Most secondary text is 14 px grey. Every
   secondary action is the same orange underlined link. Boxes use one border
   and one radius whatever they hold. Nothing on screen outranks anything else.
3. **The spacing wastes the first screen.** The masthead is 88 px, followed by
   up to 6rem of top padding. On a phone the question starts a third of the
   way down, while the controls sit mid-screen, out of thumb reach.
4. **Focus management shows the skip link after every swap.** After an HTMX
   swap the "Skip to content" link appears over the question or result. It
   shows in most study screenshots.
5. **Control styles are mixed.** Filled, outlined, borderless, and link-styled
   actions sit side by side with no rule for which is which. This is worst in
   More, Map goal actions, and Settings.
6. **Some copy leaks engineering language.** A source page showed "question
   failed strengthened_source_claim (2)". Failed preparations show "Accounted
   use: $0.500 · the final charge is unknown" in study-adjacent pages.
   Receipts say "Preparing · … Preparation stopped" in one breath.

### Stream

- Failed preparations stack as full cards above every question and never leave.
  With three failures, the question starts below the fold.
- Choice rows are plain dark boxes with no key hint. The rows look the same
  before and after a tap.
- The recall field has a heavy "Speak your answer" button between the field
  and Check. The helper line repeats what the button label already says.
- The result's labels ("Your answer", "Answer") are 14 px grey. Your answer and
  the correct answer are not laid side by side, which is the comparison that
  matters. The verdict's color is the only strong signal.
- "I was right" floats centered under a full-width slab. Next sits mid-screen
  and moves with the length of the explanation.
- More is a junk drawer: a plain button, three orange links, and a
  red-outlined Archive, in that order.
- The intro puts a 170-word note in a bordered card in a small serif. "A new
  idea" is a grey label rather than a real signal.
- Caught-up and first-run are left-aligned fragments with a stray "Check
  again" link.
- The offline banner floats over the answer choices it refers to.

### Add

- The four modes are plain radio rows with no explanation of the privacy
  difference. That difference is the reason the choice is required: Topic and
  Link reach the web, and My text never does.
- The native "Choose File · No file chosen" control shows even when Photo is
  not selected.
- The mic button is a full outlined button under the textarea.

### Map

- Failed or empty goals show as full goal cards at the top ("[fail] Quantum
  error correction · 0 questions").
- The constellation is small, its points are placed by list index rather than
  by prerequisite depth, and its lines are barely visible. It decorates without
  telling anything.
- The concept rows' status words ("new", "learning") are 14 px grey at the far
  right.
- Goal actions ("Original input", "Focus on this", "Pause") are three
  different control styles on two lines.

### Concept and Source

- The Concept page is one long scroll at a single weight. Status, the tally
  bars, the estimate, and Practice come before the note, and the "Understand
  it" heading repeats the page's purpose.
- On Source, preparation steps read as a log ("Finding ideas · Stopped")
  rather than a sequence. Citation lists are orange underlined titles.

### History, Dispute, Settings, Edit, errors

- History uses the verdict ("Not quite.") as each entry's heading, so the list
  reads as a column of verdicts. The question, which you would scan for, is
  secondary text.
- Settings is a stack of unrelated sections with inconsistent controls. For
  example, a native select sits next to a separate Save button.
- The not-found page shows two messages ("Something needs attention" and
  "Page not found").
- The Edit form is a single column of equal textareas with no grouping of
  question, answers, and evidence.

## Locked requirements

These cannot change during the redesign:

- One question, one answer control, a held result, and a deliberate Next
  (US-002).
- Exactly two masthead destinations, Add and Map. History and Settings stay
  out of the masthead.
- "I was right" sits beneath Next on an automatic miss, and "Count as a miss"
  sits in More (US-007).
- Answers are hidden before grading.
- An explicit capture mode is required, and code may preselect only the
  private modes.
- No due-count chrome, dashboard, auto-advance, or ambient animation.
- The hooks that other code depends on stay: the CSP forbids inline styles,
  there is no build step, and no remote fonts are fetched. The browser critic
  also relies on these hooks:
  - `form.answer-form`
  - `fieldset.choice-fieldset` with `.choice` buttons
  - `form[data-next]`
  - `details.overflow`
  - a `role=status` result
  - `section.empty-stage`
  - `data-state`

## Concepts

The concepts are ordered from conservative to radical. Each one names the
kind of screen it treats Scry as.

1. **Scrying glass, repaired** (conservative; Stream as a decide/learn screen).
   Keep the dark ember direction. Fix the spacing, the receipts, the control
   rules, and the result layout.
2. **Ink notebook** (evolutionary; decide/learn). Treat the page as a study
   notebook. Blue-black ink on cool paper, a red-pencil mark for misses, and a
   green tick for recall. The result is a marked answer with your answer and
   the key side by side. A reading serif is paired with a legibility sans.
3. **Observatory** (evolutionary; explore). Night-first star charts. The Map
   becomes the home of each goal's chart, with labeled stars laid out by
   prerequisite depth. The Stream is framed as looking through an eyepiece.
4. **Index cards** (structural). Each question is a card with a tab for its
   concept. The Map is a card catalog: one drawer per goal and cards per
   concept. Results "stamp" the card.
5. **Thumb zone** (structural and behavioral; operate). Built phone-first
   around the bottom third of the screen. The answer zone, Next, and More are
   anchored in a bottom sheet. The question owns the top, and nothing needed
   mid-task is out of reach.
6. **Ledger** (radical; monitor). A dense Swiss grid, with History and the Map
   as tables and recall estimates in columns.

A labeled wildcard, **Lens**, used a single luminous orb that brightens with
learning.

## Critique

The primary job is to answer one question well on a phone, understand the
idea when stuck, then see where things stand.

| Concept | Optimizes | Sacrifices | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scrying glass, repaired | Continuity, least change | Keeps both generic palettes; the operator rejected this look | Reject as a direction; keep its motion rules |
| Ink notebook | Legibility, the marked-answer moment, a subject-specific identity | Risk of skeuomorphic kitsch if paper textures creep in | **Survivor**: identity and result layout |
| Observatory | A Map that means something; keeps the "scry" metaphor | Night-only mood; star metaphors turn cute when overused | **Survivor**: the Map's star chart only |
| Index cards | Tactile clarity per question | Everything becomes an identical card (the SaaS-card trap) | Reject; keep "one sheet per question" |
| Thumb zone | Ergonomics: every mid-task control in reach | Generic app chrome if styled alone | **Survivor**: structure |
| Ledger | Density for inspection | A dashboard, which US-002 forbids | Reject |
| Lens (wildcard) | Memorability | Ambient motion and gimmick; conflicts with the motion rules | Reject |

## Synthesis

The synthesis takes each part from the concept that did it best:

- **Structure from Thumb zone.** A compact masthead. The question sits at the
  top. On a phone the answer controls and the result's Next group sit in a
  sticky bottom zone with safe-area padding. More is a consistent action list.
- **Identity from Ink notebook.** Blue-black ink on cool paper by day. Paper
  text on blue-black ink by night. Cobalt for the learner's action. A
  red-pencil color is reserved for misses and a green tick for recall. Literata
  is used for questions and reading, and Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for the
  interface.
- **The one bold element from Observatory.** Each goal on the Map gets a real
  star chart. Stars are placed by prerequisite depth, their brightness is
  concept strength, and they carry labels. It is decorative next to its
  accessible list, and it is the only ornamented element in the product.
- **The motion rules from Scrying glass.** Motion happens only in response to
  the learner, and is off under reduced motion.

Also from Index cards: each study moment is one sheet with a clear edge.
Surfaces are ranked, so sheets, rows, and chips never share one style.

## Clickable finalists

Three finalists share the synthesized structure and differ in identity. Each
shows the same question, a missed result, and a goal on the Map, using the
fixture's real content:

- [A, Ink notebook](redesign-2026-09-24/finalist-a-ink.html): cool paper and
  blue-black ink, cobalt action, Literata with Atkinson Hyperlegible Next. It
  has day and night schemes.
- [B, Observatory](redesign-2026-09-24/finalist-b-observatory.html): night
  only. A navy sky gradient and a gold primary action.
- [C, Plain](redesign-2026-09-24/finalist-c-plain.html): black and white, one
  sans family, and heavy weights.

The finalists were rendered in Chromium at 390 px in both schemes.

- **B** loses the daylight scheme, which is useful for reading on a phone
  outdoors. It also turns every surface into the metaphor, so the star chart
  stops being special.
- **C** is the most legible at a glance. But it loses the reading voice that
  notes and explanations need, and it could be any app.
- **A** keeps a reading serif for questions and notes, and a legibility sans
  for controls. It keeps both schemes. It leaves room for one bold element:
  the star chart as a window of night sky in either scheme.

## Decision

The implementation adopts **Finalist A** with these refinements:

- The star chart always renders as night sky, even in daylight, so it is the
  single ornament.
- Choice keys (1 to 4) appear as quiet labels. They also match the keyboard
  shortcuts.
- On a phone, the answer zone and the result actions are anchored to the
  bottom. On wide screens they follow the question and nothing is pinned.
- Stopped preparations collapse into one notice on the Stream and one
  "Couldn't prepare" list on the Map.

B and C remain on record. Because the structure is shared, either one could
be adopted later by changing tokens rather than rebuilding templates.
